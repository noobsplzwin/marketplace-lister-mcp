# Marketplace listing copy

## The one thing to understand first

The grid a buyer scrolls shows **photo, title, price**. Nothing else. So:

- **Title + cover photo + price earn the click.**
- **Description earns the message.** Nobody reads it until they've already clicked.

Write them for those two different jobs. A description packed with specs cannot rescue a title nobody clicks.

## Title formula

```
[Brand] [Product] ([Color] / [Size] / [Measurement]) — [Condition], $[Price]
```

Rules, in priority order:

1. **Brand first.** It is the most-searched word and it front-loads value.
2. **Include the one field buyers filter on** for that category (see below). Its absence is why a listing gets skipped.
3. **State condition in the title.** Sellers who do this consistently outperform; it is the most commonly omitted field.
4. **Use the word buyers type, not the word the manufacturer uses.** See below.
5. Price in the title is optional. Facebook already shows it, so it buys emphasis rather than information.

Most competing listings are titled `Chair`, `Dell monitor`, `coffee table`. A specific title both stands out in the grid and matches far more searches. There is no downside to length.

## The searchable-word rule

**Title the item the way a buyer would search for it, then add the manufacturer's term if it differs.**

Measured on a real local market:

- Searching `dutch oven` returned 26 listings. **Zero** said "French Oven."
- Searching `french oven` didn't work at all. Facebook doesn't recognize the phrase, degrades to matching "oven", and returns ranges, toaster ovens, and a toy oven.

Staub officially calls its product an "Essential French Oven." A listing titled that way is invisible to everyone searching "dutch oven," which is everyone. Correct title:

```
Staub 3.6L Cast Iron Dutch Oven (24cm, Cherry Red) — Like New, Made in France
```

Same trap elsewhere: buyers search "couch" more than "sofa", "TV" more than "television", "pressure cooker" alongside "Instant Pot". When two words compete, put the common one in the title and the precise one in the description. Keep model names that are genuinely searched (Instant Pot, Chuck Taylor).

## Per-category guidance

The category decides both **which field must be in the title** and **how long the description should be**.

### Full-length description (5–6 lines)

Items where the buyer needs to picture owning it, or is spending enough to want reassurance: **clothing, shoes, bikes, furniture, jewellery, anything over ~$200.**

Five beats:

1. `Selling my [full product name with the specifics repeated]`
2. One concrete sensory or design detail: `chic gold buckle`, `super soft`, `clean soles and canvas`
3. `Condition:` followed by what is *specifically* fine: `no stains, tears, or major signs of wear`
4. Where it fits the buyer's life: `pairs perfectly with jeans, skirts, or office outfits`
5. Logistics: pickup area, payment preference

Repeating size and price here is fine. It answers the question before it's asked.

Title fields that matter most:
- **Clothing**: size, color, material
- **Shoes**: size in both US and cm; buyers cross-check cm
- **Bikes**: frame size, frame material, bike type
- **Furniture**: dimensions, material, and any feature that solves a problem (storage, adjustable, extends)

### Short description (2–3 lines)

Standardized goods where the model number *is* the argument and the buyer already knows what it does: **kitchen appliances, electronics, tools, board games.**

Structure: what it is and its key spec → condition and what's included → logistics. No use-case paragraph; someone searching "Instant Pot" knows what one does.

Title fields that matter most:
- **Kitchen appliances**: capacity (cups, quarts, litres) and model number
- **Electronics / monitors**: screen size in inches, model number, headline spec, e.g. `ASUS VG248QG 24" 165Hz Gaming Monitor | G-SYNC Compatible`
- **Cookware**: the common noun, size, material
- **Board games**: exact game name + "Board Game" + completeness (`all pieces`, `sealed`)

State the retail price when the gap is the selling point (`$150 new on Amazon`). That is information, not puffery.

## Voice

Write like a person, not a catalogue: warm, specific, first-person (`Selling my…`).

Keep concrete warmth (`super soft`, `cozy`, `timeless`). It reads human and it works. Cut the abstract kind (`high-quality craftsmanship`, `perfect for any home`). It reads like filler and buyers skip it.

Avoid phrasings that mark text as machine-written, because a bot-sounding listing kills trust in a peer-to-peer sale:

- No `Not just X, but Y` constructions
- No stacked adjective triples (`sleek, modern, and versatile`)
- No `boasts`, `showcases`, `elevate`, `must-have`, `look no further`
- No opening with `Are you looking for…`

Honesty converts. Naming a flaw plainly (`small chip on the lid, doesn't affect use`) prevents the wasted trip that ends in no sale, and it makes everything else in the listing credible.

## Checklist before publishing

- [ ] Brand in the title
- [ ] The category's key field in the title (size / capacity / dimensions / frame size)
- [ ] Condition in the title
- [ ] Title uses the word a buyer would search, not only the manufacturer's term
- [ ] Description length matches the category
- [ ] Any flaw named plainly
- [ ] Pickup area and payment note
- [ ] Cover photo is the clearest full view of the item

## Worked examples

Three listings posted the same hour by the same seller, same photo quality, differing mainly in title:

| Title as published | Clicks | Problem | Better |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Cuckoo CR-0675F 6-cup micom rice cooker` | 33 | No condition; `micom` is jargon | `Cuckoo CR-0675F 6-Cup Micom Rice Cooker — Like New, $150 New` |
| `Staub Essential French Oven 24cm 3.6L cast iron, cherry red` | 11 | Invisible to `dutch oven` searches; specs cluttered | `Staub 3.6L Cast Iron Dutch Oven (24cm, Cherry Red) — Like New, Made in France` |
| `Instant Pot Lux 6 Qt 6-in-1 pressure cooker` | 4 | Nothing distinguishes it from 26 local competitors | `Instant Pot Lux 6Qt Pressure Cooker — Works Perfectly, All Parts Included` |
